⌕ Digital Forensics Laboratory
A dedicated ScamSathi-inspired investigation workspace: extract indicators from pasted artefacts, fingerprint text, and preserve the results for case follow-up. This is a triage aid, not a full forensic suite.
FORENSIC TRIAGE

1 · Artefact / IOC extraction

Paste message text, email headers, chat excerpts, or log snippets. Extract candidate URLs, domains, email addresses, IPs, hashes, and phone-like strings.

Extracted indicators will appear here.

2 · Text fingerprinting

Generate reproducible hashes for the exact text you paste. For file evidence, use the evidence upload workflow, which hashes the uploaded file bytes.

SHA-256 and SHA-1 results will appear here.

3 · Evidence handling checklist

Preserve the original file or message export; work on a copy.
Record who collected it, when, from which source, and any transfer between handlers.
Keep hashes with the evidence record and verify after transfers.
Do not open suspicious links or execute unknown attachments during triage.
Treat extracted IPs/domains/hashes as leads; do not claim attribution from indicators alone.

4 · Investigation workflow

1
Acquire
Record source and preserve originals.
2
Fingerprint
Calculate SHA-256 and verify integrity.
3
Examine
Extract candidate indicators and correlate with the scan.

Add timeline events and export the case report.

Limitations: no disk imaging, memory acquisition, malware detonation, or court-grade chain-of-custody certification is provided.

🛡 Protection & First Response
Practical next steps after a suspected scam. Always verify contact details independently.

If you have not interacted

1. Do not click the link, scan unknown QR codes again, or install an app from the message.
2. Do not share OTPs, PINs, passwords, card details, or recovery codes.
3. Open the official app or type the known website address yourself.
4. Preserve the original message, sender details, timestamps, and screenshots.

If money or account access is at risk

Contact your bank or payment provider using the number in its official app or card.
Change affected passwords from a trusted device and revoke suspicious sessions.
In India, for suspected financial cyber fraud, contact the national cybercrime helpline 1930 and verify the current reporting process through official government channels.
Do not pay anyone who promises guaranteed recovery of lost money.

ScamShield is a first-response aid, not a substitute for your bank, law enforcement, or official cybercrime reporting services.
